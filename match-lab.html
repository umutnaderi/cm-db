      Give a placed player the ball, then ask the engine what it would attempt. The choice of action (pass vs. through ball vs. cross vs. dribble vs. carry vs. shoot vs. hold-up play) is new, Match-Lab-only logic — there's no callable "what would the engine try" function to call, so this can't reuse one (see MATCH_LAB_PLAN.md — Spatial Decision Intelligence v1 scores concrete candidates by real geometry: distance, pressure, lane obstruction). Once an action is chosen, its resolution always calls the real, unmodified match engine.

      Each probe resolves the outcome of an action you've already picked below — a cross that's already been delivered, a pass that's already arrived. It doesn't ask the engine to choose the action itself; that's what Free Play does.

      Whoever you assign to a role here is a forced participant in the probe, regardless of where you place them on the pitch or which team they're on — unlike Free Play, marker distance and team don't remove or add anyone. Free Play's ball-ownership controls are hidden in this mode because they don't mean anything here: the ball is drawn at whichever placed player fills the scenario's own primary role below.
